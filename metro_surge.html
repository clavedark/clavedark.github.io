Timeline of Events — Dec 2025 to Jan 2026

Key events from the announcement of Operation Metro Surge through the end of January 2026
Dec 4, 2025
DHS announces Operation Metro Surge, deploying federal immigration agents to Minneapolis–St. Paul. Officially framed as targeting fraud in Somali-American community services.
Dec 9
~200 protesters rally in Burnsville against ICE raids. Early protests focus on deportation flights at Signature Airlines.
Dec 14
Postal workers in Minneapolis demand ICE off postal property after USPS parking lots are used as staging grounds for operations in South Minneapolis.
Dec 15
Protest against the violent arrest of a pregnant woman by ICE agents in Minneapolis.
Jan 6, 2026
DHS announces massive expansion: 2,000+ ICE agents, 1,000+ CBP officers now deployed. Called “the largest immigration enforcement operation ever carried out.”
Jan 7
Renée Nicole Macklin Good, 37, is fatally shot in her car by ICE agent Jonathan Ross. Spontaneous vigils and protests erupt immediately; federal agents deploy chemical irritants and paintballs against mourners.
Jan 8–9
Statewide outrage. 2,500 gather in Minneapolis; 2,000 attend a vigil in St. Paul. Protests spread to Rochester, Mankato, Champlin, Cloquet, Moorhead. 29 arrested at Graduate Hotel confrontation (ICE lodging).
Jan 10
“ICE Out For Good” — coordinated actions in 20+ MN cities. An estimated 20,000 march on Lake Street in Minneapolis, the largest protest in Minnesota since George Floyd.
Jan 14
Protesters encircle ICE agents; fireworks thrown; ICE vehicle broken into and documents taken. 6 protesters injured.
Jan 18
Protesters interrupt church service at 1524 Summit Ave, St. Paul. 3 arrested. 225 march in Minneapolis.
Jan 20
“Free America Walkout” — 693 events across all 50 states + DC and Puerto Rico. 20 walkouts in Minnesota alone, from high schools to factories.
Jan 23
Statewide strike / “economic blackout.” 10,000 march in Minneapolis. 100 clergy members arrested picketing the airport deportation terminal. Hundreds of businesses close in solidarity.
Jan 24
Alex Jeffrey Pretti, 37, an ICU nurse, is fatally shot by ICE agents while filming a protest on Nicollet Avenue. Video shows him coming to the aid of a woman being pushed by officers.
Jan 25
Federal agents deploy tear gas at Minneapolis protest; an agent points a weapon at a protester. Hotel vandalism at ICE lodging in Maple Grove; ICE agents moon protesters from hotel windows.
Jan 28
MN Attorney General Keith Ellison files federal lawsuit. Judge Patrick Schiltz finds ICE violated at least 96 court orders in January alone. “Hands Off MN” rallies in 4 cities.
Jan 30–31
“National Shut Down” — 317 events across 48 states. 6,000 march in Minneapolis. Judge declines to immediately halt the operation but notes “profound and heartbreaking” community harm and evidence of racial profiling and excessive force.

Notable & Creative Tactics

The Metro Surge protests produced a distinctive repertoire — from creative economic pressure to direct confrontation
Creative
Bought and returned salt at Target — because salt melts ICE
Economic pressure · 20 events
Sit-ins at Target stores statewide, protesting the company’s $2M donation to the Trump inauguration. Staged sit-in at Target HQ demanding to see CEO.
Civil disobedience
100 clergy members knelt in prayer at the airport deportation terminal on Jan 23; all were arrested. Jan 18: protesters interrupted a church service at 1524 Summit Ave.
Disruption
Noise demos outside ICE agent hotels — air horns, whistles, and drumming to disturb agents’ sleep at the Graduate Hotel and Maple Grove lodging.
Direct action
Protesters encircled ICE agents making an arrest (Jan 14). Broke into an ICE vehicle and stole documents. Agents responded with chemical irritants.
Blockade
Dragged dumpsters into streets to block ICE vehicle movement. Tried to physically block ICE vehicles from leaving arrest scenes.
Very Minnesota
Protesters threw snowballs at federal agents — plus ice and snow. Fireworks also thrown at agents on Jan 14.
Theatrical
Giant puppets and costumes at marches. Human banner formations. Silent protest at a senior living community.
