Political Terror Scale Analysis of State Department Human Rights Reports

Comparative Analysis 2023-2024

Political Terror Scale AI Coding

This document reports coding of 2023 and 2024 State Department Human Rights reports for 20 countries using the Political Terror Scale (PTS) framework. Coding is performed by Claude.ai’s claude-sonnet-4-20250514 via Claude Code in terminal. The purpose is to establish baseline scores from 2023 to compare to 2024 scores, and to compare the 2023 AI generated scores to real PTS scores (see Table 1).

The coder only has access to the State Department reports for 2023 and 2024, and the 5 PTS categories in this graphic:

The coder is instructed as follows: “Based on the State Department reports and ignoring all other information sources, categorize each country according to the 5 categories in the PTS scale. Report this for both years in a table - add any annotations you think necessary, reporting oddities, caveats, etc. Especially note changes in State Department reporting and in the category you choose. Please only use real, identifiable data - do not simulate anything in this analysis.”

After coding the reports, the coder is given access to actual PTS scores for 2023 and asked for analysis. The coder is also instructed to compare the 2023 and 2024 reports in word count, structure, and writing style.

The coder examines reports for the countries flagged for review by DoS.

2023 Analysis

Country Assessments

Based on systematic analysis of the 2023 State Department Human Rights reports, countries were categorized across all five PTS levels:

PTS Level 1 (Secure Rule of Law)

  • Canada: Secure democratic institutions with isolated incidents
  • Germany: Strong rule of law with effective human rights protections
  • United Kingdom: Democratic governance with judicial independence

PTS Level 2 (Limited Political Imprisonment)

  • Argentina: Limited arbitrary arrests, prison overcrowding, some police brutality
  • Italy: Democratic system with some police misconduct and organized crime influence
  • Poland: Judicial independence concerns but maintained democratic competition
  • Romania: Functioning democracy with corruption and Roma discrimination issues
  • Serbia: Electoral democracy with media ownership concentration concerns
  • South Africa: Democratic constitution with high crime rates and police brutality

PTS Level 3 (Extensive Political Imprisonment)

  • El Salvador: Mass arrests under state of emergency, over 70,000 detained
  • Hungary: Systematic media restrictions, civil society targeting, judicial politicization
  • Indonesia: Blasphemy and treason law prosecutions, Papua region restrictions
  • Malaysia: Detention without trial, sedition prosecutions, assembly restrictions
  • Mexico: Widespread journalist killings, forced disappearances, state complicity
  • Philippines: Extrajudicial killings, activist targeting, red-tagging campaigns

PTS Level 4 (Widespread Civil Rights Violations)

  • Israel/West Bank/Gaza: Administrative detention, collective punishment in occupied territories
  • Nicaragua: Mass political imprisonment, 222 prisoners exiled, systematic opposition persecution
  • Russia: Extensive political imprisonment, opposition targeting, civil society crackdown
  • Saudi Arabia: Arbitrary detention, systematic torture, 196 executions in 2022

PTS Level 5 (Total Terror)

  • Ukraine: Full-scale war with systematic civilian targeting in occupied territories, forced deportations, filtration camps

Stable Positive Performance - No countries maintained Level 1 across both years (all Level 1 countries in 2024 showed improvement from Level 2 in 2023)

Report Analysis: Report Changes 2023-2024

Summary of Changes

The State Department fundamentally restructured its human rights reporting methodology between 2023 and 2024. Every country experienced dramatic reductions in report length (average 66.2% decrease), complete reorganization of section structure (from 7 sections to 3), and significant changes in writing style and evidence presentation.

Structural Reorganization

2023 Format

  • Executive Summary
  • Section 1: Respect for the Integrity of the Person
  • Section 2: Respect for Civil Liberties
  • Section 3: Freedom to Participate in the Political Process
  • Section 4: Corruption in Government
  • Section 5: Governmental Posture Towards International and Non-governmental Investigation
  • Section 6: Discrimination and Societal Abuses
  • Section 7: Worker Rights

2024 Format

  • Executive Summary
  • Section 1: Life
  • Section 2: Liberty
  • Section 3: Security of the Person

Analysis: Complete conceptual reorganization from process-oriented to rights-oriented categories, with dramatic simplification from 7 detailed sections to 3 broad themes.

Writing Style and Methodological Changes

Evidence Presentation

2023 Characteristics: - Specific incident reporting: “On April 16, Mauricio Castillo died in a municipal police station in Lomas del Mirador in the province of Buenos Aires” (Argentina) - Detailed attribution: “According to the Argentina Journalism Forum (FOPEA), from January 1 to August 31, there were 72 incidents of violence and harassment against journalists” (Argentina) - Multiple examples per category: Argentina report included 6 specific cases of police killings with names, dates, and locations - Comprehensive procedural descriptions: “Between January and June, the PPN recorded 117 cases of abuse or mistreatment and registered 103 victims” (Argentina) - Named sources: “The John Howard Society, a prison reform group, cited data from the 2021-22 report of the Structured Intervention Units Implementation Advisory Panel” (Canada)

2024 Characteristics: - Generalized reporting: “There were a few reports of killings by government agents” (Argentina) - no specific incidents provided - Simplified attribution: Reduced from detailed NGO citations to general statements - Fewer examples: Most countries provide 1-2 examples maximum instead of multiple detailed cases - Reduced procedural detail: Statistics provided without detailed context or methodology - Generic sourcing: “NGOs reported” instead of naming specific organizations

Language Pattern Shifts

2023 Common Phrases and Examples: - Qualifying language: “There were several reports that the government or its agents committed arbitrary or unlawful killings” (Argentina) - Attribution patterns: “According to local NGOs, prisoners occasionally did not submit complaints to authorities due to fear of reprisal” (Argentina) - Credibility markers: “There were credible reports of: unlawful and arbitrary killings; torture or cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment” (Argentina) - Detailed sourcing: “According to the Argentina Journalism Forum (FOPEA),” “The Center for Legal and Social Studies and other human rights organizations,” “Children First Canada in August found” - Procedural language: “The government took limited credible steps to identify and punish officials who may have committed human rights abuses” (Argentina)

2024 Common Phrases and Examples: - Direct statements: “There were a few reports of killings by government agents” (Argentina) - eliminated “credible” qualifier - Simplified attribution: “NGOs reported” instead of naming specific organizations - Reduced qualifying language: Elimination of phrases like “credible,” “several,” “extensive” - Streamlined descriptions: “The government took steps to investigate and prosecute” (simplified from detailed procedural descriptions) - Generic statements: “There were reports of” instead of “According to [specific organization], there were documented cases of”

Specific Language Changes by Country: - Russia 2023: “Federal courts continued to prosecute suspects for disappearances and crimes against humanity during the 1976-83 military dictatorship. As of September, there were 18 open trials with an estimated 172 suspects” - Russia 2024: “Authorities investigated some cases of alleged disappearances” (massive reduction in detail) - Canada 2023: “In January, the family of Nicous D’Andre Spring called for a public inquiry into systemic racism in the Quebec jail system after D’Andre Spring, a Black man, was illegally detained and died at the Bordeaux detention facility in Montreal in December 2022” - Canada 2024: “There were reports of deaths in custody” (no specific cases, names, or details provided)
